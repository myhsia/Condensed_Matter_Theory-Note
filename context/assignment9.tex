\section{Band structure of graphene}

\begin{problem}
  In the calculation of the monolayer graphene band structure using
  tight-binding model, if we keep the contribution of the next-nearest
  hopping $t'$.
  \begin{enumext}
    \item Write down the dispersion relation.
    \item Expand the energy spectrum around the Dirac point up to second order.
    Search the literature and check what's trigonal warping effect is in
    a monolayer.
  \end{enumext}
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}\leavevmode
  \begin{enumext}
    \item The Hamiltonian in second quantization form for the graphene with
    the next-nearest (NN) hopping is
    \[
      \mathcal H
    = -t\sum_{\braket<i,j>} (\hat a_i\hat b_j^\dagger + \text{H.c.})
    - t'\sum_{\ab*<\braket<i,j>>}
      (\hat a_i\hat a_j^\dagger + \hat b_i\hat b_j^\dagger+ \text{H.c.})
    = \mathcal H_t + \mathcal H_{t'},
    \]
    where $i$ and $j$ label the nearest neighbor, i.e., the bond $A_i, B_i$.
    \begin{paracol}{2}
    The fermion operators $a_i$ and $a_i^\dagger$ annihilate/create an electron
    at site $A$, also for $b_i$ and $b_i^\dagger$ at site $B$. The vectors
    pointing from $A_i$ to the 3 nearest $B$-type sites and 6 next-nearest
    $A$-type sites are
    \[\begin{array}{*3{l@{\enspace}}}
      \bm \delta_1 = a(1, 0), &
      \bm \delta_2 = a\ab(-\frac12,  \frac{\sqrt3}{2}), &
      \bm \delta_3 = a\ab(-\frac12, -\frac{\sqrt3}{2}); \\
      \bm a_1 = a\ab(\frac32, \frac{\sqrt3}{2}), &
      \bm a_2 = a(0, \sqrt3), &
      \bm a_3 = a\ab(-\frac32, \frac{\sqrt3}{2}), \\
      \bm a_4 = a\ab(-\frac32, -\frac{\sqrt3}{2}), &
      \bm a_5 = a(0, -\sqrt3), &
      \bm a_6 = a\ab(\frac32, -\frac{\sqrt3}{2}).
    \end{array}\]
    Assume there are $2N$ atoms in total, then there are $N$ $A$-type or
    $B$-type sites in total.
    \switchcolumn \vspace*\fill \centering
    \begin{tikzpicture}[scale = 1.2]
      \foreach \i in {0,120,240}
        \draw [rotate around = {\i:(0,0)}]
              (0,0) --++ (0:1) --++ (-60:1) --++ (-120:1) --++
              (-180:1) --++ (-240:1) --++ (60:1);
      \foreach \i in {0,120,240}
        {
          \shade [rotate around = {\i:(0,0)}, ball color = red]
            (0,{sqrt(3)}) circle (.1);
          \shade [rotate around = {\i:(0,0)}, ball color = red]
            (1.5,{sqrt(3)/2}) circle (.1);
          \shade [rotate around = {\i:(0,0)}, ball color = blue]
            (1,0) circle (.1);
          \shade [rotate around = {\i:(0,0)}, ball color = blue]
            (1,{-sqrt(3)}) circle (.1);
        }
      \shade [ball color = red] (0,0) circle (.1);
      \draw [opacity = .3, dashed, <->] (30:.1) to [bend right]
                    node [above] {$t'$} (30:{sqrt(3) - .1});
      \draw [opacity = .3, dashed, <->] (90:.1)
                        to [bend right] (90:{sqrt(3) - .1});
      \draw [opacity = .3, dashed, <->] (150:.1)
                        to [bend right] (150:{sqrt(3) - .1});
      \draw [opacity = .3, dashed, <->] (120:.1)
                        to [bend right] (210:{sqrt(3) - .1});
      \draw [opacity = .3, dashed, <->] (270:.1)
                        to [bend right] (270:{sqrt(3) - .1});
      \draw [opacity = .3, dashed, <->] (-30:.1)
                        to [bend right] (-30:{sqrt(3) - .1});
      \draw [opacity = .6, <->] (30:.1) to[bend left]  ([shift = (150:.1)]0:1);
      \draw [opacity = .6, <->] (150:.1) to[bend left]
        ([shift = (270:.1)]120:1);
      \draw [opacity = .6, <->] (-90:.1) to[bend left]
        node [midway, right] {$t$} ([shift = (30:.1)]240:1);
      \draw [->, very thick, teal] (0,0) -- (0:1)
       node [below, near end] {$\bm\delta_1$};
      \draw [->, very thick, teal] (0,0) -- (120:1)
       node [right, near end] {$\bm\delta_2$} ;
      \draw [->, very thick, teal] (0,0) -- (240:1)
       node [left, near end]  {$\bm\delta_3$};
      \node [left] at (0,0) {$A_i$} node [left] at (0,{sqrt(3)}) {$A_j$};
      \node [above left] at (120:1) {$B_i$} node [right] at (60:2) {$B_j$};
      \draw [->, orange] (0,0) -- (30:{sqrt(3)})  node [right] {$\bm a_1$};
      \draw [->, orange] (0,0) -- (90:{sqrt(3)})  node [above] {$\bm a_2$};
      \draw [->, orange] (0,0) -- (150:{sqrt(3)}) node [above]  {$\bm a_3$};
      \draw [->, orange] (0,0) -- (210:{sqrt(3)}) node [below] {$\bm a_4$};
      \draw [->, orange] (0,0) -- (270:{sqrt(3)}) node [below] {$\bm a_5$};
      \draw [->, orange] (0,0) -- (330:{sqrt(3)}) node [right] {$\bm a_6$};
    \end{tikzpicture}
    \vspace*\fill
    \end{paracol}
    Applying the Fourier transformation to the fermion
    operators
    \[\begin{array}{
      >\displaystyle l@{~}>\displaystyle c@{~}
      >\displaystyle l@{\quad}
      >\displaystyle l@{~}>\displaystyle c@{~}
      >\displaystyle l}
      \hat a_i &
  = & \frac1{\sqrt N}
      \sum_{\bm k} \upe^{\iu\bm k \cdot \bm r_i} \hat a_{\bm k}, &
      \hat b_{i+\bm\delta} &
  = & \frac1{\sqrt N} \sum_{\bm k,j}
      \upe^{\iu\bm k \cdot (\bm r_i + \bm\delta_j)} \hat b_{\bm k},\\
      \hat a_i^\dagger &
  = & \frac1{\sqrt N} \sum_{\bm k}
      \upe^{-\iu\bm k \cdot \bm r_i} \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger, &
      \hat b_{i+\bm\delta}^\dagger &
  = & \frac1{\sqrt N} \sum_{\bm k,j}
      \upe^{-\iu\bm k \cdot (\bm r_i + \bm\delta_j)} \hat b_{\bm k}^\dagger.
    \end{array}
    \]
    Consider the first term of the Hamiltonian, it can be expressed as
    \begin{multline*}
      \mathcal H_t = -t \sum_{i\in A} \sum_{j=1}^3 \ab(
        \frac1N \sum_{\bm k} \upe^{-\iu \bm k \cdot \bm r_i}
        \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger
        \sum_{\bm k'} \upe^{\iu \bm k' \cdot (\bm r_i+\bm\delta_j)}
        \hat b_{\bm k'} + \text{h.c.})\\
      = -t\frac1N \sum_{\bm k, \bm k'} \sum_{i\in A}
        \upe^{\iu(\bm k'-\bm k) \cdot \bm r_i}
        \sum_{j=1}^3 \upe^{\iu \bm k' \cdot \bm\delta_j}
        \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k'} + \text{h.c.}
      = -t\sum_{\bm k}
        \sum_{j=1}^3 \upe^{\iu \bm k \cdot \bm\delta_j}
        \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k} + \text{h.c.}
    \end{multline*}
    where $\sum_{i\in A} \upe^{\iu(\bm k'-\bm k)\cdot\bm r_i}
      = N\delta_{\bm k',\bm k}$ is applied, and multiple sum will not
      occur here since we only sum over every $A$-type site.

    Denote $\Delta(\bm k) = -t\sum_{j=1}^3 \upe^{\iu\bm k \cdot \bm \delta_j}$,
    this part becomes
    \[
      \mathcal H_t
    = \sum_{\bm k} \Delta(\bm k) \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k}
    + \sum_{\bm k} \Delta^*(\bm k) \hat b_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat a_{\bm k}
    \]
    Similarly for the second part: Denote $\epsilon(\bm k)
    = -t'\sum_{m=1}^6 \upe^{\iu \bm k \cdot \bm a_m}$, one can obtain
    (The Hermit conjugate is applied, but the factor $\frac12$ is also applied
    to prevent a twice sum between the same NN bond.)
    \[
      \mathcal H_{t'}
    = \sum_{\bm k} \epsilon(\bm k) \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat a_{\bm k}
    + \sum_{\bm k} \epsilon(\bm k) \hat b_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k}.
    \]
    Here the $\epsilon(\bm k)$ should not taken Hermit conjugate, since the
    hopping $A_i$-$A_j$ and $B_i$-$B_j$ are the same physical process.
    Hence, the total Hamiltonian is
    \begin{multline*}
      \mathcal H = \mathcal H_t + \mathcal H_{t'}
    = \sum_{\bm k} \ab(
        \Delta(\bm k)   \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k} +
        \Delta^*(\bm k) \hat b_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat a_{\bm k} +
        \epsilon(\bm k) \hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat a_{\bm k} +
        \epsilon(\bm k) \hat b_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k})\\
    = \sum_{\bm k} \ab(\hat a_{\bm k}^\dagger \hat b_{\bm k}^\dagger)
      \begin{pmatrix}
        \epsilon(\bm k) & \Delta(\bm k)\\
        \Delta^*(\bm k) & \epsilon(\bm k)
      \end{pmatrix}
      \begin{pmatrix}
        \hat a_{\bm k}\\
        \hat b_{\bm k}
      \end{pmatrix}
    = \sum_{\bm k} \bm\Psi^\dagger \mathbf H_{2\times2} \bm\Psi
    \end{multline*}
    By taking $\det(\mathbf H_{2\times2} - E\identity) = 0$, one can obtain the
    dispersion relation
    \[
      (\epsilon(\bm k) - E)^2 = \Delta(\bm k) \Delta^*(\bm k), \quad
      E_\pm = \pm\sqrt{\Delta(\bm k)\Delta^*(\bm k)} + \epsilon(\bm k)
    \]
    To obtain the exact expression, expanding $\Delta(\bm k)$
    and $\epsilon(\bm k)$
    \[\begin{array}{l@{~}c@{~}l}
      \Delta(\bm k) & = &
      -t\ab[\upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a(1,0)}
          + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a\ab\big(-\frac12, \frac{\sqrt3}{2})}
          + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a\ab\big(-\frac12,-\frac{\sqrt3}{2})}]\\
  & = & -t\ab(\upe^{\iu k_xa}
            + \upe^{-\frac12\iu k_xa} \cdot \upe^{\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\iu k_ya}
            + \upe^{-\frac12\iu k_xa} \cdot \upe^{-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\iu k_ya})\\
  & = & -t\upe^{-\iu k_xa} \ab[1 + 2\upe^{\frac32\iu k_xa}
          \cos\ab(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}k_ya)],\\[1ex]
      \Delta(\bm k) \Delta^*(\bm k) & = &
      t^2\ab\bigg[3
        + \underbrace{2\cos(\sqrt3k_ya)
        + 4\cos\ab(\frac32k_xa)\cos\ab\bigg(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}k_ya)
        }_{f(\bm k)}],\\
      \epsilon(\bm k) & = &
     -t'\bigg[\upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a\ab(\frac32,\frac{\sqrt3}{2})}
          + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a(0,\sqrt3)}
          + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a\ab(-\frac32,\frac{\sqrt3}{2})}\\
      & & + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a\ab(-\frac32,-\frac{\sqrt3}{2})}
          + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a(0,-\sqrt3)}
          + \upe^{\iu\bm k\cdot a\ab(\frac32,-\frac{\sqrt3}{2})}\bigg]\\
    & = & -t'\ab[\upe^{\sqrt3\iu k_ya}
              + 2\upe^{\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\iu k_ya}  \cos\ab(\frac32k_xa)
              + 2\upe^{-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\iu k_ya} \cos\ab(\frac32k_xa)
              + \upe^{-\sqrt3\iu k_ya}]\\
    & = & -t'\ab[2\cos(\sqrt3k_ya)
      + 4\cos\ab(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}k_ya) \cos\ab(\frac32k_xa)]
      =   -t'f(\bm k)
    \end{array}\]
    Substituting them back to the dispersion relation, we can obtain
    \[
      E_\pm = \pm t\sqrt{3 + f(\bm k)} - t'f(\bm k)
    \]
    with $f(\bm k)$ defined above.
    \item Take the Dirac point $\bm K = \frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt3a}(\sqrt3,1)$, then
    calculate $f(\bm K + \bm q)$ ($|\bm q| \ll 1$).
    \begin{multline*}
      f(\bm K + \bm q)
    = 2\cos(\sqrt3 K_ya + \sqrt3 q_ya)\\
    + 4\cos\ab(\frac32 K_xa + \frac32 q_xa)
      \cos\ab(\frac{\sqrt3}{2} K_ya + \frac{\sqrt3}{2} q_ya)
    = -3 + \frac94|\bm q|^2a^2,
    \end{multline*}
    where the identities for the trigonometric functions and the approximations
    $\cos(x) \sim 1 - \frac12 x^2$, $\sin x \sim x$ are used.
    Substituting $f(\bm K + \bm q)$ to $E_\pm$
    \[
      E_\pm = \pm t\sqrt{3 - 3 + \frac94|\bm q|^2a^2}
              - t'\ab(-3 + \frac94|\bm q|^2a^2) + \mathcal O(|\bm q|^3)
    = \pm\frac{3t}{2}|\bm q| a + 3t' - \frac94t'|\bm q|^2a^2
      + \mathcal O(|\bm q|^3).
    \]
  \end{enumext}
\end{solution}

\begin{problem}
  Graphene has the record room-temperature electron mobility, mainly due to two
  reasons. The first is the high Debye temperature ($\sim\qty{2100}\K$) that
  suppresses phonon scattering arising from the in-plane covalent $sp^2$ bonding
  between carbon atoms. The second one is related to its unique linear Dirac
  band, resulting in massless Dirac fermions ($m^* = 0$) near the Dirac point.
  However, if we apply the definition of the effective
  mass $m^* = \hbar^2\ab(\odv[2]{E(k)}k)^{-1}$ to the band dispersion of
  graphene $(E_\pm(k) = \pm \hbar v_Fk)$, we will get diverging $m^*$ and
  non-differentiable Dirac point. Please explain this contradiction.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
  The contradiction arises from applying different definitions of ``mass'' to
  graphene's unique band structure. The description of ``massless Dirac
  fermions'' comes from the analogy to relativistic particles.
  \par \vspace{.5\baselineskip}
  From a massless special relativity particle like a photon to the electrons in
  the graphene, their dispersion relations have the comparasion
  \[
    E = pc \Longrightarrow E = \pm \hbar v_F k = p v_F
  \]
  with $v_F$ playing the role of $c$. Hence, electrons near the Dirac point
  behave as if they have zero rest mass.
  \par \vspace{.5\baselineskip}
  However, the conventional effective mass $m^* = \hbar^2 (\odv[2]E/k)^{-1}$ is
  defined under the parabolic-band approximation $E \propto k^2$. For graphene's
  linear dispersion, $\odv[2]E/k = 0$ away from $k = 0$, leading
  to $m^* \to \infty$ mathematically. At $k = 0$ the dispersion is
  non-differentiable, so the formula breaks down.
  \par \vspace{.5\baselineskip}
  Physically, this infinite effective mass reflects that the electron's speed is
  constant at $v_F$: An external force parallel to its momentum cannot change
  its speed, much like a photon's speed is fixed at $c$. Thus, the ``massless''
  label refers to the relativistic analogy, while the divergent $m^*$ indicates
  the inapplicability of the parabolic effective mass concept to a linear band.
\end{solution}
